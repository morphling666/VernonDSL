Future compiler architecture / worked case study

From semantic graph choices to simulatable physical plans

The compiler does not claim to invent distributed algorithms or novel fusion algorithms. Humans, rules, solvers, search procedures, and agents propose bounded alternatives. The compiler composes and verifies those alternatives, lowers their compute regions to portable tile-level semantics, and only then forms complete physical-plan candidates that an external Simulation Engine can compare.

Authority boundary: the Program and semantic contracts define what must be preserved. Candidate generators propose how to distribute, represent, fuse, and implement it. The verifier defines the legal set. A replaceable Simulation Engine predicts whole-plan behavior but never makes an illegal plan legal.
The critical ordering is deliberate. A joint graph plan is not yet a physical plan: high-level fusion regions do not expose enough local computation and data movement to predict kernel performance. After distribution and representation choices are expanded, the compiler lowers each compute region to Portable Tile IR. A physical plan then combines those tile programs with communication, transfer, placement, dependency, and resource events.

Generate and verify

Propose bounded graph choices, propagate their consequences, and reject violations of semantic, distribution, numerical, memory, or effect contracts.

Make plans evaluable

Lower compute and fused regions to portable tile-level semantics before claiming to predict their performance.

Evaluate externally

Send complete physical plans to analytical models, event simulators, vendor tools, learned models, or measurements through one extensible interface.

1 / End-to-end compiler architecture

Physical plans are formed only after portable tile semantics exist

The compiler owns semantic composition, verification, tile semantic lowering, and the candidate protocol; proposal mechanisms remain extensible. Evaluation is delegated through a stable protocol to a replaceable Simulation Engine, which may be maintained by this project, another system, or a hardware vendor.

2 / Operator semantics and compute fusion

Operators expose provable semantics from which the compiler derives compute fusion

The planner does not infer behavior from names such as matmul, softmax, or attention. Operators expose semantic facts, compositional algebra, and exceptional constraints—not implementation hints such as “use FlashAttention” or “tile=128.” Unknown information creates a conservative barrier rather than fabricated semantics.

The general theory: fusion is a schedule transformation

Fusion is not justified by recognizing adjacent operator names. In the dependence and polyhedral formulation, each operation S has an iteration domain DS, read and write relations from iterations to logical data coordinates, and a schedule θS that assigns each dynamic operation instance a lexicographic logical time. Accesses to the same logical coordinate induce true, anti-, and output dependences; effects and explicit semantic ordering add further dependence edges.

D S dynamic instances of operation S R S , o ⊆ D S × I o read relation from instances to coordinates of object o W S , o ⊆ D S × I o write relation from instances to coordinates of object o
Δ S → T , o = { ( x , y ) ∈ D S × D T | ∃ a : ( x , a ) ∈ W S , o ∧ ( y , a ) ∈ R T , o }
∀ ( x , y ) ∈ Δ required , θ S ( x ) ≺ lex θ T ( y )

A fusion candidate replaces the original schedules and may eliminate an intermediate materialization. It is accepted only when the following sufficient proof obligations all hold. For affine or Presburger domains and accesses, relation composition, image/preimage, emptiness, and lexicographic-order checks can discharge these obligations exactly. General data-dependent accesses use a richer domain-specific relation or a conservative unknown; the compiler does not pretend that every operator is polyhedral.

1. Coverage and value mapping

Every required source instance is executed exactly once, or is deliberately recomputed under a valid refinement. Boundary Values and Storage versions map to the same observable results.

2. Dependence preservation

Every required RAW, WAR, WAW, effect, alias, publication, and explicit-order edge remains lexicographically forward in the fused schedule.

3. Region availability

For each consumer tile, access-relation preimages identify the exact producer instances and data regions that must be complete before consumption.

4. Reduction completion

A nonlinear consumer cannot observe a partial reduction. Reordering or distributed combination requires declared identity, associativity, commutativity, and ordering laws.

5. Effects and lifetime

Fusion preserves aliases, visible side effects, synchronization, ownership, other users, and the lifetime of any intermediate whose materialization is removed.

6. Numerical refinement

The new evaluation order, precision, overflow, rounding, and exceptional-value behavior must satisfy the declared numerical and reproducibility policy.

Sufficient-condition guarantee: if the fused implementation preserves boundary coverage and value mapping, orders every required dependence, consumes only complete required regions, and satisfies effect, alias, reduction, and numerical contracts, then it is observationally equivalent at the SemanticRegion boundary. Failure to prove any obligation rejects the candidate; profitability is a separate question.

Operators should not expose

GPU ranks, collective-library names, warps or CTAs, tile sizes, shared-memory swizzles, CUDA intrinsics, stream IDs, predicted runtime, or strategies named TP or Flash. These belong to topology profiles, physical schedules, backend capabilities, or cost profiles.

Opaque operators remain valid

They still require typed boundaries, effects and aliases, capabilities, conservative cost, reference behavior, and fallback. Missing access or algebra information makes an operator a partition and fusion barrier; backend code is never used to reconstruct semantics.

Strict derivation: tiled matmul followed by ReLU

Consider a contraction that produces C and a pointwise consumer that produces O. The derivation below uses only the contracts and proof obligations above.

D M = { ( i , j , k ) ∈ ℤ 3 | 0 ≤ i < M , 0 ≤ j < N , 0 ≤ k < K } D R = { ( i , j ) ∈ ℤ 2 | 0 ≤ i < M , 0 ≤ j < N } 
Producer M:
  reads  A[i,k], B[k,j]
  reduction contribution: A[i,k] * B[k,j]
  combiner: floating-point add under NumericalPolicy
  writes completed C[i,j] after all k

Consumer R:
  reads  C[i,j]
  writes O[i,j] = max(C[i,j], 0)
Δ M → R = { ( ( i , j , k ) , ( i , j ) ) | 0 ≤ k < K } T R = I × J P M ( T R ) = { ( i , j , k ) | i ∈ I , j ∈ J , 0 ≤ k < K }

Discharging the obligations

1. Tile the consumerChoose T_R = I × J. Its read relation requires exactly C[I,J].
2. Compute the producer preimageInverting the producer write map for C[I,J] yields I × J × [0,K). The full reduction dimension is required; this is a relation calculation, not a pattern guess.
3. Construct the fused scheduleFor each (i,j) ∈ I × J, execute all required k contributions, complete the reduction into a private tile value, then execute ReLU. Thus every edge in Δ_(M→R) remains forward.
4. Prove materialization removalIf C[I,J] has no external user, observable storage identity, alias, or publication effect, the private completed tile supplies the same Value and the global C write/read pair may be removed.
5. Check numerical policyThe candidate preserves the declared order of the k reduction, then applies ReLU once. It does not move ReLU into the reduction.
6. Form the physical regionThe accepted FusionRegion contains the producer preimage, completed tile value, and consumer tile, with an allowed split between completed matmul and ReLU. Tile scheduling remains a later profitability decision.
Legal fused order for each output tile:
  for i in I, j in J:
    c = reduction_identity
    for k in [0,K):
      c = combine(c, A[i,k] * B[k,j])
    O[i,j] = relu(c)

Rejected order:
  for k in [0,K):
    c = relu(combine(c, A[i,k] * B[k,j]))

Reason:
  the nonlinear consumer observes incomplete reduction states, and in general
  relu(Σ x_k) ≠ Σ relu(x_k).
Where attention differs: replacing materialized softmax with an online AttentionSummary(m,l,o) is not implied by generic fusion theory. It is an algorithmic refinement with a separately declared and verified combine law. Once that equivalence is available, the same coverage, dependence, completion, effect, and numerical obligations derive legal tiled, split-K, paged, or streamed fusion candidates. Without the refinement proof, the compiler must retain materialized softmax or another validated fallback.

3.1 / Communication-aware fusion

Communication can fuse with compute and conversion only after it becomes explicit

Each partition candidate first materializes required redistribution, then undergoes local fusion and tile evaluation. Communication tasks produce region-availability tokens, so consumers need not wait for an entire tensor.

3.2 / Mixed precision and explicit conversions

Mixed precision introduces explicit quantization, dequantization, metadata, and numerical constraints

A Value may use distinct storage, transfer, compute, and accumulation representations. Changing one affects HBM and network traffic, conversion work, tile shapes, error, and fusion opportunities.

Deterministic path

Every portable tile program has a legal baseline implementation path. The system remains correct and portable when no agent, autotuner, or specialized backend is available.

Optimized path

Rules, autotuners, agents, or external systems may rewrite Portable Tile IR or lower it directly to a kernel DSL, target IR, library call, or artifact. Translation validation preserves the same tile semantics.

Portable Tile IR also makes proposed hardware evaluable before silicon exists. A hardware team can supply a hypothetical HardwareSpec and simulator, search legal implementations internally, and compare compute, memory, precision, and interconnect choices over the same workload suite.
5 / External Simulation Engine

The compiler standardizes evaluation queries without requiring one built-in cost model

Joint optimization requires a shared whole-plan objective, but the evaluator need not be implemented inside the compiler. The compiler sends a complete PhysicalPlan to an external SimulationEngine. The engine may orchestrate analytical models, portable-Tile-IR predictors, network simulators, profile databases, vendor tools, learned models, and real measurements.

Pluggable evaluation providers

Fast pruning

Symbolic bounds, roofline estimates, memory accounting, capability checks, and conservative communication formulas reject clearly dominated plans.

Plan simulation

Tile predictors and event or network simulators model local compute, communication, contention, overlap, staging, and critical paths.

High-fidelity evidence

Compilation, vendor simulators, cycle-level models, profile lookup, microbenchmarks, or complete execution calibrate a small set of promising candidates.

Extensibility boundary: the compiler owns the typed query, semantic provenance, units, objectives, and required result fields. Each Simulation Engine provider owns its prediction method and declares applicability, uncertainty, unsupported features, version, and calibration evidence. A missing provider reduces optimization quality; it does not prevent deterministic compilation.

Portable Tile IR makes prediction possible; it does not make prediction exact. An engine may internally search legal implementation families, use analytical envelopes or profile analogues, delegate to a hardware simulator, or compile and measure. It must report the method and uncertainty so that estimates produced at different fidelity levels are not treated as equally authoritative.

Factorized joint optimization

1. Generate macro alternativesInstantiate known distributed strategies, logical meshes, placements, boundary representations, fusion regions, stage cuts, and recomputation choices.
2. Expand and prunePropagate partitions and representations, materialize redistribution and conversions, verify legality, and retain a top-K frontier rather than one early winner.
3. Lower tile semanticsConvert every surviving compute region to Portable Tile IR and assemble complete PhysicalPlan candidates.
4. Evaluate at increasing fidelityUse cheap bounds broadly, simulation for the reduced frontier, and compilation or measurement only where its value exceeds its cost.
5. Feed evidence backIf tile feasibility, workspace, communication progress, numerical error, or measurement changes the ranking, revise only the affected decisions.
Meaning of joint: partition, physical placement, mixed precision, and fusion share one whole-plan evaluation loop. Meaning of factorized: candidate generation and verification remain staged so that the compiler never attempts an intractable flat Cartesian search.

6.3 / Case study: supplied attention refinement

From standard softmax attention to a composable block state

This section does not introduce a primitive compiler operator. It starts from standard attention and derives the established online-softmax state used by blockwise attention algorithms. The equations are established algorithmic machinery; the name AttentionSummary is only this document's IR abstraction for carrying that state through partitioning, fusion, and tile lowering.

Provenance: online maximum and normalizer updates come from established online-softmax work and are used by FlashAttention's blockwise algorithm. Summary(m,l,o) is not a standardized industry IR operation and is not inferred automatically from the name attention. See Online normalizer calculation for softmax and FlashAttention.
Central consequence: online summary exposes softmax attention as a block reduction over the key/value axis. Matching K/V blocks can therefore be processed sequentially or in parallel, stored locally or remotely, and accessed contiguously or through pages. Tiled, Split-K, ring, streamed, and paged implementations are different choices along those physical dimensions—not five mutually exclusive algorithms.

1. Begin with ordinary attention

For one query vector q, key kj, and value vj, let sj be the masked and scaled attention score. The output is a softmax-weighted sum of all values:

sj = q·kj d +maskj O = ∑jall keys exp(sj) vj ∑jall keys exp(sj)

A materialized implementation writes the score matrix and softmax probabilities before multiplying by V. A blockwise implementation instead visits subsets of keys and retains only enough state to produce the same final result.

2. Define the state for one key/value block

Let C be any subset or block of key/value positions. Stable softmax first subtracts the largest score in that block. Define:

mC = maxj∈C sj lC = ∑j∈C exp(sj− mC) oC = ∑j∈C exp(sj− mC) vj OC = oClC

m: stability scalar

The maximum score for this query in the block. Subtracting it prevents overflow without changing the normalized result.

l: denominator scalar

The block's softmax normalizer after maximum subtraction.

o: numerator vector

The unnormalized weighted sum of value vectors. Lowercase o is not the final attention output; final O is o/l.

This document packages those three quantities as AttentionSummary<FP32>(m,l,o). The package name is compiler terminology introduced here; the underlying online-softmax state is established.

3. Combine independently computed blocks

Suppose blocks A and B were evaluated independently. Their exponentials use different local maxima, so their l and o values cannot be added directly. First rescale both blocks to the maximum of their union:

Block A
(mₐ, lₐ, oₐ)
⊕
Block B
(mᵦ, lᵦ, oᵦ)
→
A ∪ B
(m, l, o)
m= max(mA, mB) α= exp(mA−m) β= exp(mB−m) l= αlA+ βlB o= αoA+ βoB Ofinal= ol

The rescaling follows directly from exp(s − m) = exp(s − mA) · exp(mA − m) for elements in block A, and likewise for B. Consequently the combined state represents exactly the union of both blocks in real arithmetic. Floating-point reassociation and accumulation order still require the declared numerical policy.

4. Small numerical example

scores = [1, 2, 3]
values = [10, 20, 30]          // scalar values only to keep the example short

Block A = first two positions:
  mA = 2
  lA = exp(-1) + 1             ≈ 1.3679
  oA = 10·exp(-1) + 20         ≈ 23.6788

Block B = final position:
  mB = 3, lB = 1, oB = 30

Combine using m = 3, α = exp(-1), β = 1:
  l  ≈ 1.5032
  o  ≈ 38.710
  O  = o / l                   ≈ 25.75

This equals the ordinary softmax-weighted sum over all three positions.

5. The reduction structure exposed to the compiler

Once a domain library or agent supplies this refinement and its validation contract, the key-position dimension becomes a declared reduction whose element is a matching K/V block and whose partial result is AttentionSummary: 

for each query row or query tile:
  partials = []
  for each matching key/value block C:
    partials.append(make_summary(Q, K[C], V[C]))
  final_summary = reduce(combine_summary, partials)
  O = final_summary.o / final_summary.l

K and V must be partitioned together along the same key-position set: K[j] produces score s[j], and V[j] supplies that score's value contribution. Splitting the query axis produces independent outputs and needs no summary combination. Splitting the key axis divides this reduction and therefore produces partial summaries that must be combined.

Fallback boundary: online summary is a supplied algorithmic refinement, not a consequence of generic fusion. Without its registered equivalence and numerical contract, the compiler must retain materialized softmax or another independently validated implementation.

6. Orthogonal physical choices enabled by the reduction

Semantic implementation

Materialized: write complete score and probability matrices, then compute PV. This conservative path does not require online summary.

Online: retain only block state and avoid complete S/P materialization.

Local traversal

Tiled fused: one worker visits local K/V blocks, updates an on-chip summary, and fuses QK, mask, online update, and PV. The traversal may be sequential even though the reduction is decomposable.

Reduction parallelism

Split-K: multiple workers process different K/V ranges for the same query. Each emits a PartialValue<AttentionSummary>; an explicit reduction combines them.

Distributed movement

Ring or streamed KV: K/V blocks reside on different devices and move through a supplied route. A query owner updates its summary whenever a block becomes available.

Storage and addressing

Paged KV: a page table selects non-contiguous K/V blocks. Each gathered page contributes to the same reduction; page loading and dequantization may join the tile region.

Representation

K/V blocks may use BF16, FP8, or INT4 storage and transfer while the summary remains FP32. Every conversion, scale, error constraint, and communication byte remains explicit.

7. These choices compose into physical candidates

Local prefill

online summary
+ local tiled traversal
+ contiguous K/V
+ fused QK/mask/update/PV

A FlashAttention-style local candidate.

Long-KV decode

online summary
+ paged K/V
+ quantized page loads
+ Split-K workers
+ summary reduction

Storage layout and reduction parallelism are combined rather than selected as alternatives.

Distributed long context

online summary
+ context-partitioned K/V
+ ring or hierarchical stream
+ receive/dequant/update fusion
+ optional local paging

Placement, route, local storage, precision, and fusion jointly define the physical plan.

The compiler enumerates only combinations made available by CandidateGenerators, verifies their distribution and numerical obligations, lowers each surviving local update to Portable Tile IR, and asks the Simulation Engine to compare complete physical plans.

Concrete decisions in this candidate

Distribution and placement

Each (stage, replica) group occupies one eight-GPU node. Dense attention uses TP=4 × CP=2 inside that node. The MoE region redistributes to EP=8 on the same node. Pipeline transfers cross nodes.

Representations

Residual and normalization boundaries use BF16. Supported dense and expert matrix operands use FP8 with FP32 accumulation. Pipeline, streamed-KV, and MoE-dispatch payloads use FP8 with explicit scales. Online-attention state remains FP32.

Fusion and communication

QKV epilogues produce the selected transfer layout. KV receive, dequantization, and online-summary update form one portable tile region. TP output uses reduce-scatter. MoE pack, transfer, unpack, and grouped-GEMM remain explicit dependent tasks with chunk availability.

7 / Conclusions and limits

The framework composes and evaluates supplied algorithms; it does not promise to invent them

Compiler-owned mechanisms

  • Instantiate and compose supplied partition, placement, precision, fusion, and schedule candidates;
  • Propagate shardings and representations and materialize redistribution and conversion;
  • Verify effects, partial values, fusion, numerical policy, capacity, and progress;
  • Lower all compute regions to Portable Tile IR and assemble complete physical plans;
  • Query replaceable Simulation Engines and retain qualified variants with deterministic fallbacks.

Not promised by the core compiler

  • Inventing TP, CP, EP, pipeline schedules, online attention, or novel fusion algorithms;
  • Inferring aliases, effects, numerical equivalence, or visibility from operator names;
  • Obtaining accurate performance from high-level FusionRegion alone, before tile lowering;
  • Allowing a Simulation Engine score or an agent assertion to override legality;
  • Requiring one built-in cost model or treating one strategy as optimal for every workload.
Precise claim: supplied algorithms become typed CandidateGenerators with explicit preconditions and verification obligations. The compiler can then compose partition, placement, mixed precision, and fusion decisions; expose their consequences; lower surviving compute regions to Portable Tile IR; and compare complete physical plans through an external, replaceable Simulation Engine.
